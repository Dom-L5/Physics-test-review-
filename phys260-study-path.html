PHYS 260 · Exam 1 / Formula & recognition sheet

Chapters 5–6 · k = 1/(4πε₀), Coulomb constant; ε₀ = vacuum permittivity. Numerical constants supplied separately.

Charge & force

Q=ne;F=k|q1q2|r2

n is a signed integer; e > 0. Electron count N = |Q|/e. Like signs repel; unlike attract. r is separation.

Point-charge field

E=k|Q|r2;F→=qE→

Q is source charge; q is test charge. E away from +Q, toward −Q. Negative q reverses force.

Coordinates → components

Ex=kQΔxr3;Ey=kQΔyr3

Δx = xP − xQ, Δy = yP − yQ; r = √(Δx² + Δy²). Use signed Q. x̂, ŷ, ẑ are unit directions.

Superposition

E→=∑iE→i;E=Ex2+Ey2

Add signed components separately. Eₓ = E cos θ, Eᵧ = E sin θ for θ from +x. Determine angle quadrant from signs.

Charge densities

λ=QL;σ=QA;ρ=QV

Uniform: λ (C/m), σ (C/m²), ρ (C/m³). L = length, A = area, V = volume. dq = λ dl, σ dA, or ρ dV.

Continuous distributions

E→=k∫dqr2r^

r runs from each charge element to P; r̂ points that way. Pair symmetric elements before integrating.

Very long uniform line

E=|λ|2πε0r=2k|λ|r

r = perpendicular distance. Away from positive, toward negative. Length ≫ r; far from ends. Gaussian cylinder: area 2πrL.

Uniform finite rod: midpoint axis

E=k|λ|Lzz2+(L/2)2

L = full length, z > 0 perpendicular to midpoint. z ≪ L → long line. z ≫ L → point charge Q = λL.

Uniform ring: axial field

Ez=kQz(z2+R2)3/2

R = radius; signed z = axial distance from center; signed Q. Transverse fields cancel. Center E = 0.

Uniform disk: axial field

Ez=σ2ε0(1−zz2+R2)

R = radius, z > 0. Other side: reverse direction for same |z|. Q = σπR². Near face → sheet; far → point charge.

Dipole in a uniform field

p→=qd→;τ→=p→×E→;τ=pEsinθ

q > 0 is either charge magnitude. d is full separation, from − to +. θ is between p and E. Net force = 0; torque may not be.

Flux through a flat patch

ΦE=E→·A→=EAcosθ

Uniform E, flat area A. A→=An^, normal to patch. θ is from the normal, not the plane. Closed surfaces use outward normals.

Gauss’s law: every closed surface

∮E→·dA→=Qencε0

Qenc = algebraic charge inside only. External charge affects local E, not net flux. Zero flux does not imply E = 0.

Spherical symmetry

Er=kQencr2;A=4πr2

Concentric Gaussian sphere; Eᵣ is signed outward field. Symmetry belongs to the source, not just the chosen surface.

Uniform insulating solid sphere

Qenc=Qr3R3;Er=kQrR3(r<R)

R = source radius, r = observation radius. Outside: Eᵣ = kQ/r². Interior E grows linearly with r.

Infinite nonconducting sheet

E=|σ|2ε0;2EA=σAε0(σ>0)

Pillbox: two caps each A; enclosed charge σA. E away from +σ, toward −σ. No distance dependence.

Conducting material: equilibrium

E→metal=0;Enormal,out=σε0

Field just outside is normal; σ is local surface density. Excess charge is on surfaces. Charged cavities need not have E = 0.

Cavity charge bookkeeping

Qinner=−q;Qouter=Qc+q

q = total cavity charge; Qc = isolated conductor net charge. Off-center charges make inner density nonuniform. Radial formulas require symmetry.

Before calculating: geometry → source signs → arrows → components → SI units. Before finishing: vector direction, units, limiting case. Fields: N/C; forces: N; flux: N·m²/C; dipole moment: C·m; torque: N·m. Convert cm → m, nC → 10⁻⁹ C, μC → 10⁻⁶ C. Conductor shell: zero net inner charge does not imply a uniform inner surface or zero cavity field.
